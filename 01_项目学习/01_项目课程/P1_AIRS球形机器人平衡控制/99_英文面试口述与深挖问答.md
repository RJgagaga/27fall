## 一、口述

### 1.1 30 秒

> At AIRS I worked on balance control for a three-omni-wheel ballbot with a dual-arm upper body. I built a cascaded controller — a position loop giving bounded tilt references to an attitude loop, allocated to the wheels through the kinematics — and tuned it on the robot. I also derived a linearised inverted-pendulum model and used LQR to analyse the tuning, added a centre-of-mass feedforward from the URDF, and implemented the safety loop: initialisation, output limits and fall handling.

（中文：三全向轮 ballbot；级联控制、实机整定；LQR 分析；质心前馈；安全闭环。）

### 1.2 90 秒

> My internship at AIRS, from September 2025 to March 2026, was on a ballbot: a robot balancing on one ball, driven by three omni wheels, with two arms on top. The hard part is that it is unstable by itself, and when the arms move the centre of mass moves too.
>
> I was responsible for balance control. I used the IMU and wheel-speed feedback in a cascade: a position outer loop produces a bounded tilt reference, an attitude inner loop follows it using the angle and the gyro rate, and the output is allocated to the three wheels through the ball-wheel kinematics. I deployed and tuned it on the real robot.
>
> When tuning alone could not explain the behaviour, I went back to a model: a four-state linearised inverted pendulum, with LQR to study how the state weights trade stability against control effort. That became our reference for reading the deployed gains and for finding state anomalies and actuator saturation.
>
> For the arms, I estimated the centre-of-mass offset from the joint angles with Pinocchio and the URDF, fed a gravity feedforward into roll and pitch, and checked it by switching it on and off under fixed arm trajectories. Finally I made the loop safe: state initialisation, output limiting and fall detection.

### 1.3 3 分钟（在 90 秒基础上加深）

> （接 1.2）Let me add three details. First, why a cascade: to accelerate, a ballbot has to lean, so the tilt angle is effectively an acceleration request. The outer loop turns position and velocity errors into a tilt reference, bounded so that the acceleration stays in the range the linear model and the motors can handle; the inner loop uses the gyro rate as damping.
>
> Second, the model work. I derived the equations with the Lagrange method, linearised around upright, and got a four-state model. It is non-minimum phase — to lean forward, the ball first has to move back — which limits how aggressive the position loop can be. With LQR I swept the weights and saw how a higher tilt weight raises the gains and the peak torque. I expanded the cascade into an equivalent state feedback to compare it with the LQR gains. I did not deploy LQR; it was an analysis tool.
>
> Third, the feedforward and safety. The arm pose shifts the true balance point, so the feedforward effectively moves the attitude set-point. I verified it by switching it on and off with identical arm trajectories. For safety, the controller starts disabled, waits for valid sensor data, captures offsets, resets integrators and clears the references, clamps its outputs, and detects excessive tilt. The result: the chassis stayed balanced while both arms moved along their trajectories, which was the target my mentor set.

## 二、问答（每题：答案 + 中文要点 + 追问 L2/L3）

### A. 项目总览

**A1. Tell me about the project in one sentence.** → 见 1.1。

**A2. What is a ballbot and why is it hard?**
> It balances on a single ball driven by three omni wheels; it is an inverted pendulum with one contact point and no passive support, so it must be actively stabilised in two tilt directions at once.

中文：单球、倒立摆、两个方向同时稳。
L2: *How fast does it fall?* — "Like e to the lambda t; for an effective length of 0.4 m — an illustrative figure — it doubles roughly every 0.14 s."
L3: *So what does that imply for your loop?* — "Sensor and filter delay must stay well below that, and the control rate must be much faster."

**A3. What exactly was your responsibility?**
> Balance control development and on-robot tuning, including the safety logic. Arm drivers, hands and mechanical design belonged to others.

L2: *Which part are you most proud of?* — "Going back to the model when tuning stalled." L3: *What would you do differently?* — "Log more from the start and automate the regression tests." 

**A4. How did you know it worked?**
> The chassis kept dynamic balance while both arms executed fixed trajectories, which was the objective set by my mentor. For the feedforward I compared on/off runs with the same arm trajectory, five runs each. With it off, the peak tilt was about 0.06 radians, around 3.4 degrees, and the RMS tilt about 0.022. With it on, the peak dropped to about 0.026 radians and the RMS to about 0.010, and the wheel commands hit the limit in about 1 percent of samples instead of 6.

L2: *Which metrics?* — peak and RMS tilt, hold-phase offset, drift, saturation fraction. L3: *Numbers?* — use the honest template.

### B. 运动学

**B1. How do you map ball motion to the three wheels?**
> Each wheel drives the ball along its tangential direction. The ball's surface velocity at the contact is omega cross r; projecting it on the drive direction and dividing by the wheel radius gives the wheel speed. Stacking three wheels gives a 3×3 matrix J; each row is the wheel's axle direction scaled by R over r.

L2: *Singularities?* — contact latitude 0 or 90°; best conditioning at about 54.7° where the axes are orthogonal. L3: *What is yours?* — "About 45 degrees, with the three wheels 120 degrees apart; the ball radius is 0.2 metres and the wheel radius 0.05, so the ratio is 4."

**B2. Velocity vs torque distribution?**
> Velocities map with J, torques with J inverse transpose, by power conservation. I must not feed a torque into a velocity map.

**B3. How do you estimate the robot's speed?**
> Wheel speeds → inverse of J → ball rate relative to the body → v = R ω → rotate by yaw → integrate. It is only a local estimate: slip, body rotation and drift matter.

L2: *Why body rotation?* — wheels measure relative to the body; add the gyro rate. L3: *Impact?* — e.g. 0.2 rad/s tilt rate on 3 rad/s is ~6% (illustrative).

**B4. Clip each wheel or scale the vector?**
> Scale the whole vector: the map is linear, so the ball's direction is preserved.

**B5. How did you check the kinematics before powering the robot?**
> Single-axis tests with the ball lifted: pure yaw gives three equal wheel speeds, pure forward rolling leaves one wheel still, and feeding wheel speeds back through the inverse recovers the input. This catches unit, order and sign errors.

### C. IMU / 状态

**C1. Which signals do you use from the IMU?** → roll/pitch for error, gyro for damping (答案同第 03 章 Q1)。
**C2. Who does the sensor fusion?**
> The module's onboard AHRS gives the orientation; my driver-side work was parsing, frame conversion and validity checks. I did not write my own attitude filter; the only filtering I added was a light low-pass on the gyro, around 25 hertz, and a slower one on the wheel-speed velocity estimate.

L2: *Frame conversion?* — FRD/NED to FLU. L3: *Why does the roll channel have a minus sign?* — "Positive roll tips the body toward −y, so a leftward error needs negative roll."

**C3. How do you handle IMU zero offset?**
> I capture roll and pitch at enable with the robot held upright and subtract them; otherwise it balances around a wrong zero and drifts.

L2: *Quantify?* — "In my model the drift is about the gain ratio times the offset: roughly 0.2 m per degree for the illustrative gains." L3: *Re-enable?* — "Every enable re-captures the offset: 200 samples, about two seconds at 100 hertz, with the robot held still; I discard the batch if the gyro reads more than about 0.05 rad/s."

**C4. Does filtering matter?**
> Yes. A first-order filter's delay is about its time constant; below a hertz of cutoff adds fractions of a second of lag into a loop with an unstable pole of several rad/s. I filter the gyro lightly, around 25 hertz, and leave the angle alone. The only heavy filter is 0.8 hertz on the wheel-speed velocity used by the outer loop, which has a bandwidth of about 0.15 hertz, so that delay of roughly 0.2 seconds is acceptable there.

**C5. How do you make sure the data are valid?**
> Check quaternion norm, CRC at the driver, message age against a threshold, and that all sources have arrived before enabling.

### D. 建模与 LQR

**D1. Walk me through the model.** → 第 04 章 Q1。
**D2. Why linearise and when does it fail?** → 第 04 章 Q2。
**D3. What is non-minimum phase here?**
> The torque-to-position transfer function has a right-half-plane zero: to lean forward, the ball first moves back. It limits the achievable bandwidth and causes an initial undershoot — about 6 cm in my illustrative simulation.

**D4. What did you use LQR for?** → 第 05 章 Q1。
**D5. How exactly did it guide PID tuning?** → 第 05 章 Q2。
**D6. How did you choose Q and R?** → 第 05 章 Q3。
**D7. Why not deploy LQR?** → 第 05 章 Q4。
**D8. Saturation diagnosis?** → 第 05 章 Q5。
**D9. Continuous or discrete LQR?**
> I designed in continuous time; at 100 Hz the discrete gains differ by about 5% in my illustrative model, so the conclusions hold. For deployment I would use the discrete Riccati equation.

L2: *Computational cost?* — "Solving Riccati is offline; online it is four multiply-adds." L3: *So why not?* — model scope, tunability, limits and anti-windup.

**D10. How would LQR change if the arms are extended?**
> The inertia and centre-of-mass height change, so the optimal gains change; I would either tune for a compromise or schedule the gains by arm pose.

### E. 级联 PID

**E1. Why a cascade?** → 第 06 章 Q1。
**E2. Why bounded reference?** → 第 06 章 Q2；补充：bounded tilt = bounded acceleration ≈ θmax·g/(1+R/l).
**E3. What does the D term do?** → 第 06 章 Q3。
**E4. Torque or velocity output?** → 第 06 章 Q4。
**E5. Anti-windup?** → 第 06 章 Q5。
**E6. How do you tune it on the robot?**
> Safety first: lifted or on a gantry, low gains, e-stop. Check signs, then inner loop: raise P until slight oscillation, back off, add D with the gyro and watch the noise; then outer loop: velocity damping first then position; then the bounds; then feedforward and arm motion; and regression-test after each change.

L2: *What if it oscillates slowly?* — outer loop too fast relative to inner; reduce outer gain or bound. L3: *And buzzing?* — D noise or too little filtering; check gyro noise times Kd.

**E7. What are the relative bandwidths and rates?** > Outer ≤ 1/3–1/5 of inner; sample ≥ 10–30× bandwidth. In our case the control loop ran at 100 hertz, the inner attitude loop had a bandwidth of about 1 hertz, the outer position loop about 0.15 hertz, and the motor driver's speed loop about 20 hertz.

**E8. Why does the robot first move backward when you command forward?** → 非最小相位；球后拨使机体前倾。

### F. 质心前馈

**F1. How did you get the CoM offset?** → 第 07 章 Q1。
**F2. Sign logic?** → 第 07 章 Q2。
**F3. Why feedforward?** → 第 07 章 Q3。
**F4. How did you verify?** → 第 07 章 Q4（数字用诚实模板）。
**F5. Limits?** → 第 07 章 Q5。
**F6. How is the gain set?**
> It is an empirical gain because the controller output is not a physical torque. I would calibrate it with static arm poses by regressing the steady correction against the computed moment.

**F7. What happens if the joint order is wrong?** > Wrong links get wrong angles, so the centre of mass and the feedforward are wrong; I map by joint name and assert all names are found.

### G. 安全与系统

**G1. What does your safety loop contain?**
> Start disabled; on enable wait for valid IMU and wheel data, capture offsets, reset integrators, filters and references, and send zero. Clamp outputs. Detect excessive tilt and stop the output; require a deliberate re-enable. Concretely: a fall is roll or pitch above 0.5 radians for five consecutive cycles; the output goes to zero, the controller resets, and it latches in a fault state until a person re-enables it.

L2: *What triggers a fall?* — "Tilt above 0.5 radians, about 29 degrees, for five cycles. Also stale IMU or wheel data older than 50 milliseconds, NaN values, or the wheel commands sitting at the limit for half a second." L3: *How do you know the motors stop?* — "I would check the whole path: zero command, driver timeout, motor behaviour; I'd rather describe what I verified than claim more."

**G2. How do you deal with timing in ROS?**
> Sensors arrive asynchronously; the control timer reads the latest cache; I check data age with timestamps and measure loop period and jitter.

**G3. What is the ROS/CAN chain?** > Controller publishes wheel commands; a driver node packs them into CAN frames; feedback returns as joint states. CAN ran at 1 Mbit/s and the wheel feedback came back at about 100 hertz, in step with the controller.

**G4. What happens when a command stops arriving?** > A good design has a watchdog in the driver that zeros the output; ours was 100 milliseconds, and feedback missing for more than 50 milliseconds was treated as a motor-offline fault.

**G5. How did you debug?** → 见第 09 章，说一个真实案例（STAR）。

### H. 软技能

**H1. What was the hardest part?** > Non-minimum phase and the interplay of loops; tuning without a model stalled until I derived one.
**H2. How did you work with your mentor?** > Concise updates, precise questions, independent once agreed.
**H3. What did you learn?** > Model-based analysis complements tuning; verify on hardware; safety is a design item.
**H4. Why a master's?** > To add systematic theory (control, learning-based methods) to my hands-on skills; link to PS.

## 三、STAR 案例

### STAR 1：回到模型分析

- **S**：Tuning the cascade by hand stopped explaining the robot's behaviour; gains were hard to justify. 
- **T**：Find a principled way to read the gains and the saturation. 
- **A**：Derived a four-state linearised model, ran LQR with several weights, expanded the PID into equivalent state feedback and compared. 
- **R**：It gave the team a reference for gains, state anomalies and saturation (mentor letter). One concrete decision it changed: the model showed that the inner-loop damping gain should be about half the proportional gain, and that a larger tilt reference raises the peak control effort sharply. So I raised the damping gain, which removed a slow 1.5-hertz oscillation, and I cut the tilt-reference bound from 0.15 to 0.10 radians, which stopped the wheels from hitting their limit during pushes.

### STAR 2：质心前馈的开关验证

- **S**：Arm motion made the chassis drift/tilt. 
- **T**：Show the feedforward helps. 
- **A**：Estimated CoM with Pinocchio, added roll/pitch feedforward, ran identical fixed trajectories with it on and off. 
- **R**：Balance response improved: peak tilt fell from about 0.06 to 0.026 radians, RMS tilt from 0.022 to 0.010, drift over 30 seconds from about 0.30 to 0.10 metres, and limit-hitting from about 6 to 1 percent of samples.

### STAR 3：一次具体故障（用第 09 章案例补全）

- **S**：Early in tuning, every time I re-enabled the controller after a fall test, the robot lurched forward for a moment before settling.
- **T**：Find out why, and make re-enabling safe, because the next step was to test with the arms moving.
- **A**：I plotted the target position, the position estimate and the integrator around the enable instant. The target position and the integrator still held the values from the previous run, and the tilt offset had been captured while the robot was still being steadied by hand. I wrote the five-step initialisation: wait for valid data, capture 200 samples of offset only when the gyro is quiet, set the target to the current position, clear integrators and filters, send zero first, and ramp the output.
- **R**：The lurch disappeared in repeated enable tests, and it became our standard procedure: re-enable only after the robot is held upright, and always re-capture the offset.

中文：S 重新使能时机器人先猛冲一下；T 查原因并让重新使能安全；A 画出使能瞬间的目标、位置估计、积分，发现都是上次残留，偏置也是在被手扶晃动时采的，于是写五步初始化；R 猛冲消失，成为标准流程。
