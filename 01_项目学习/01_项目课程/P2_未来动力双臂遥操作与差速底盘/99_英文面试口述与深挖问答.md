## 1. 口述

### 30 秒

> At Shenzhen Future Power I worked on dual-arm and chassis control. I mapped VR controller motion to end-effector targets and solved constrained inverse kinematics with MuJoCo and Mink; I built leader–follower teaching with gravity compensation on the leader and position–velocity tracking on the follower; and I closed wheel-speed PID loops with odometry on a differential-drive base. Everything was validated on real hardware through a ROS 2 to CAN chain.

要点：三条线 + 真机 + ROS2-CAN。

### 90 秒

> My internship had three parts. First, VR teleoperation. The controller pose is relative: when the operator presses the trigger I store the controller pose and the end-effector pose, and afterwards the target is the start pose plus the scaled controller displacement, rotated into the robot frame. Mink turns the targets into a small quadratic program each cycle, with frame tasks for both hands, a low-weight posture task, damping and limits, and I integrate the resulting joint velocity. The damping keeps joint speeds bounded near singularities.
> Second, leader–follower teaching. From the URDF I built the dynamics with KDL. The leader gets only a gravity-compensation torque in the motor's MIT frame with zero stiffness, so it is easy to drag; the follower tracks the leader's mapped position and velocity.
> Third, the base: differential-drive kinematics turn body velocity into wheel targets, encoder feedback closes a PID loop, and the wheel motion is integrated into odometry, for both teleoperated and autonomous modes.

### 3 分钟

> [Context] The goal was a dual-arm platform on a mobile base that a person could control by VR or by hand-guiding a leader arm, all through one ROS 2 to CAN execution chain.
> [VR] The key idea is relative mapping. Absolute controller coordinates would make the arm jump and may be unreachable, so a clutch stores both start poses. Position target equals the start position plus s times R_a times the controller displacement. Rotation is the controller's relative rotation, conjugated by R_a and applied by left multiplication to the start orientation; I scale translation only. The targets go to Mink as frame tasks; each cycle it solves a QP over the joint increment, with a posture task to resolve redundancy, damping, and limits, then I integrate with a 5 millisecond step. Near a stretched arm the Jacobian's smallest singular value goes to zero and the inverse would amplify velocity by one over sigma; damped least squares turns the gain into sigma over sigma squared plus lambda squared, trading a little tracking for bounded joint speed.
> [Leader–follower] The dynamics are M q-double-dot plus C q-dot plus g equals tau. In hand guiding, gravity dominates, so the leader's motors output only the gravity torque computed from URDF masses and centres of mass: kp and kd are zero, so the operator feels friction and inertia only. The follower receives mapped position and velocity with gains. The mapping handles zero offsets and direction per joint.
> [Base] Differential-drive kinematics: wheel speeds are v plus or minus omega L over two. Encoder feedback closes the PID, and the odometry integrates the arc length of each wheel.
> [Validation] I validated on real hardware, checking direction, tracking and limits through the encoders. The follower tracked the leader with an RMS joint error of about 0.01 radians, the end-effector steady error was under 5 millimetres, and the base drove a 2-metre straight line with less than 5 centimetres of sideways drift. One real fault was the base drifting sideways, which turned out to be a wheel-radius mismatch; I fixed it with straight-line and in-place rotation tests.
> [Takeaway] I learned to localise faults by layer: algorithm, ROS, driver, bus, motor.

## 2. 深挖问答

### A. 总览与职责

**1. What exactly was your role?** 
> I was the robotics software intern on dual-arm and base control. I owned the VR pose mapping and the Mink IK configuration and tuning, the gravity-compensation parameters and follower gains, and the differential-drive kinematics, wheel-speed PID and odometry integration with ROS 2. The motor driver wrapper, the STM32 CAN framework and the VR capture client already existed in the team; I read them closely and used them for validation.
中文：不夸大，分清自己与团队。

**2. Why three different control levels?** 
> Because the tasks differ: pose targets need IK, the leader must be compliant so it gets pure torque, the follower must reproduce joints so it uses position and velocity, and the base needs a speed loop.

**3. What does "validated on real hardware" involve?** 
> Commands went through ROS 2 and CAN to the motors, and I checked encoder feedback for direction, tracking and limits. The numbers I looked at were joint direction against the encoder, follower RMS error of about 0.01 radians, end-effector steady error under 5 millimetres, and that no joint touched its limit; for the base, a 2-metre straight line with under 5 centimetres of sideways drift.

**4. How did you localise faults?** 
> By layer: look at the target on the ROS topic, then the CAN frame fields, then the encoder feedback.

### B. VR 映射

**5. Why relative instead of absolute mapping?** 
> Different origins and ranges; absolute causes a jump at start and unreachable targets. A clutch stores start poses.

**6. Write the position and rotation formulas.** 
> p_d = p_r0 + s R_a (p_v − p_v0); R_d = (R_a ΔR R_a^T) R_r0 with ΔR = R_v R_v0^T.

**7. Why conjugate by R_a?** 
> The relative rotation is expressed in the VR frame; the similarity transform expresses the same rotation about the corresponding axes in the robot frame while keeping the angle.

**8. Why left multiplication?** 
> It rotates about fixed base axes; right multiplication rotates about the gripper's own axes and would roll the gripper.

**9. Why not scale the rotation matrix?** 
> It stops being orthonormal; scaling a rotation needs axis-angle or SLERP.

**10. How do you verify R_a?** 
> Orthonormal with determinant +1, and single-axis motion tests with small amplitude.

**11. Quaternion pitfalls?** 
> xyzw versus wxyz ordering, and q versus −q cancelling in linear blending.

**12. Did you use SLERP?** 
> No; the filter was a linear blend with normalisation, which is fine for small steps but needs hemisphere handling; I would flip signs or use SLERP.

**13. What does the clutch do on release?** 
> Clears the reference; re-pressing stores new references so the arm does not jump.

**14. What filter and what delay?** 
> A first-order low-pass; time constant ≈ T(1−α)/α; I balance smoothness against lag. I used alpha of about 0.3 at 100 hertz, so the time constant is about 23 milliseconds, which is smooth enough to hide hand tremor without feeling laggy.

**15. What is the TCP offset issue?** 
> The grip pose differs from the tool centre by a fixed transform; ignoring it makes rotations about the hand centre appear as rotations about the wrist.

### C. MuJoCo / Mink

**16. Why MuJoCo?** 
> It supplies the robot model, kinematics and Jacobians for Mink and a visualisation; the real arm needs joint commands, not MuJoCo.

**17. qpos, qvel, ctrl?** 
> Generalised position, velocity, and actuator inputs whose meaning depends on actuator type.

**18. Why can nq differ from nv?** 
> Quaternion joints have four position numbers but three velocity numbers, so integrate with the library.

**19. What is a FrameTask / PostureTask?** 
> Frame task: drive a frame to a target pose; posture task: low-weight preference to keep joints near a reference, resolving redundancy.

**20. Why one QP for both hands?** 
> Shared decision variable, conflicts resolved by weights, and constraints apply globally.

**21. Why a 5 ms step?** 
> It is the solver's integration period configuration, not measured loop rate; I check the real period.

**22. Do weights mean importance percentages?** 
> No; units differ (metres vs radians), they are relative preferences.

**23. What if the solver fails?** 
> The wrapper returns the current configuration for that step; it is not a safe stop, so the hardware node has its own timeout.

**24. How do you apply the IK result?** 
> Joint targets are published via ROS 2; the hardware node maps them to MIT frames.

### D. IK 与奇异

**25. What is the Jacobian?** 
> The map from joint velocities to end-effector velocity; column j is the effect of joint j alone.

**26. Why is a 7-DOF arm redundant?** 
> Six task dimensions, seven joints; null-space motion does not move the end effector.

**27. What is a singularity?** 
> Jacobian rank loss, e.g. a stretched arm.

**28. Why velocity amplification?** 
> Inverse gain is 1/σ_min; for a two-link arm 1 m/s needs 1 rad/s at 90° elbow but 22 rad/s at 5.7°.

**29. Explain damped least squares.** 
> Minimise ‖Jq̇−v‖² + λ²‖q̇‖²; gain σ/(σ²+λ²), maximum 1/(2λ).

**30. What do you pay?** 
> Tracking residual in weak directions.

**31. Is damping a joint limit?** 
> No; soft penalty versus hard constraint.

**32. How did you monitor singularity?** 
> Minimum singular value, max joint speed and task residual in the log. I flagged anything below a sigma-min of about 0.05, and the logs showed joint speed spikes only below that.

### E. 约束

**33. Which hard constraints?** 
> Position and velocity bounds on the joint increment, optional collision distance.

**34. Why not clip afterwards?** 
> Clipping changes the direction; in a small example 16° error versus 8° with the bound inside the QP.

**35. Position limit margin?** 
> Bound by gain<1 times remaining distance; approaches asymptotically.

**36. Motor-side limits?** 
> MIT ranges and clamping; unclamped packing wraps and flips torque sign (+11 → −9 N·m on a 10 N·m motor).

### F. 动力学与主从

**37. Why can the leader be pushed easily?** 
> Zero stiffness plus gravity-cancelling torque.

**38. Derive gravity torque of two links.** 
> Partial derivative of potential energy; 19.6 and 4.9 N·m at horizontal for unit links.

**39. Why gravity only?** 
> Coriolis scales with speed squared; hand guiding is slow.

**40. What did URDF/KDL give you?** 
> Masses, centres of mass and joint axes → JntToGravity.

**41. What can go wrong?** 
> Sign, joint order, zero offset, inaccurate masses.

**42. How do you test the sign?** 
> Hold horizontal, send only gravity torque, see if it falls or floats.

**43. Compensation ratio?** 
> Slightly below one is safer; 0.8 on the last joint, because the gripper and handle payload there is the least certain part of the model. I raised the ratio from 0.5 upward; above about 0.9 the joint started to drift up slowly after I let go, so I settled on 0.8 and kept a 10 to 20 percent margin.

**44. MIT fields?** 
> Position, velocity, kp, kd, feed-forward torque; τ = kp e + kd ė + τff.

**45. Does torque zero mean no force on the follower?** 
> No; the position/velocity terms still produce torque.

**46. Why send velocity?** 
> Removes lag: error bω/kp versus (b+kd)ω/kp.

**47. Follower gravity sag?** 
> g/kp without gravity feed-forward, e.g. 3.4/300 ≈ 0.6°; feed-forward removes it.

**48. How do you start safely?** 
> Align first with a smooth trajectory, limit reference rate, ramp torque down on stop.

### G. 底盘

**49. Differential-drive equations?** 
> v_r,l = v ± ωL/2.

**50. Saturation handling?** 
> Scale both wheels equally to keep curvature.

**51. Odometry equations and errors?** 
> Δs=(Δs_r+Δs_l)/2, Δψ=(Δs_r−Δs_l)/L; 1% wheel mismatch → 1.25 m lateral error after 10 m.

**52. ROS2–CAN chain and timeouts?** 
> Command topic → bridge → CAN frame → driver; feedback back as odometry; heartbeat/timeouts at several layers. The topics were /dual_xarm/joints for arm targets, /cmd_vel for the base and /odom back. The driver froze the arm after 100 milliseconds without a target and disabled it after 500; the base zeroed its wheels after 300 milliseconds without a velocity command; the board heartbeat was designed for 500 milliseconds, but reading the firmware I found its flag is never updated, so the host-side timeout is what really protects us.

**Follow-up pattern**: if asked "why?" three times, go: physical reason → equation → what you observed on the robot.

## 3. STAR 案例

**S1 — Sign-reversed gravity compensation.** 
Situation: leader arm got heavier when released. Task: find why. Action: held arm horizontal, sent gravity torque on one joint, saw it accelerate down, traced to a mapping sign. Result: flipped sign in the mapping layer, arm floated. In full: on the first hardware run the leader arm felt heavier when I let go, and it slowly sank. The task was to find out whether the model or the signs were wrong. I held the arm horizontal, commanded only the gravity torque on joint 2, and it accelerated downwards, so the torque sign was reversed against the encoder direction. The cause was a per-joint direction sign in the motor-to-joint mapping. After I corrected it, the arm stayed where I left it, and I then raised the compensation ratio from 0.5 to its final values step by step. I now check every joint this way, with torque only and the arm horizontal, before any other test.

**S2 — Near-singularity joint spike.** 
Situation: joints spun fast near full extension. Action: logged σ_min and joint velocity, raised damping and bounded target speed. Result: bounded motion with slightly slower tracking. In full: when the operator stretched the arm almost straight, joints 2 and 4 spun at over 3 radians per second for a fraction of a second. The log showed the smallest singular value falling below 0.03. I raised the damping to 1e-3, clipped the target speed to 0.5 metres per second and limited joint speed to 2.5 radians per second for joints 1 to 4. After that the peak joint speed stayed below the limit, at the cost of about 2 millimetres more tracking error near full extension.

**S3 — Odometry heading drift.** 
Situation: base drifted sideways. Action: straight-line and in-place rotation tests, corrected wheel parameters. Result: reduced drift. In full: driving straight for 2 metres, the base veered about 12 centimetres to one side. I ran straight-line and in-place rotation tests: five metres straight for the wheel radius, and ten full turns for the track width. The radius I was using was about 1.5 percent off. After calibrating both values, the sideways drift over 2 metres dropped below 5 centimetres, and the heading error over ten turns was about 30 degrees in total.
