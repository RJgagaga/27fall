## 1. 三个版本口述

### 30 秒

> I worked on a lab wheel-legged robot whose leg length can change. My part was balance control and on-robot tuning. I built a gain-scheduled LQR on body pitch and motion states, where the gains change with the leg length, and a virtual model controller that maps support force and pitch torque to the joint motors through the Jacobian. I deployed it on an STM32H7 with FreeRTOS and FDCAN, and tuned it on the real robot, so it kept balance and tracked speed commands while the legs extended and retracted.

要点：腿长可变 → 增益调度 LQR → VMC → STM32H7 实车。

### 90 秒

> The robot has two wheels and two five-bar legs, so it is an unstable system that must be actively balanced, and its dynamics change with the leg length. I was responsible for the balance algorithm and the tuning.
>
> First, state and model. I computed the virtual leg length and angle from the closed-chain kinematics, combined the leg angle with the IMU pitch, and estimated the body velocity from wheel speed, leg motion and acceleration. That gives six states: leg angle and rate, position and velocity error, body pitch and rate.
>
> Second, control. I designed LQR gains offline on a grid of leg lengths and scheduled them with polynomials in the leg length. The LQR outputs a wheel-hub torque and a hip torque. A leg-length PID with gravity feedforward gives the support force, and VMC maps the support force and the hip torque to the two joint torques with the Jacobian transpose.
>
> Third, deployment. On the STM32H7 with FreeRTOS and FDCAN I tuned the robot, and when balance failed I traced it to state inconsistency, torque limits or ground-contact transitions, and fixed each one.

### 3 分钟

> (Start with the 90-second version, then add the following three blocks.)
>
> **Why gain scheduling.** When the leg length changes, the center of mass height, the inertia and the torque lever arms change, so the linearized A and B matrices change. In my model the unstable pole scales roughly as one over the square root of the length, and the input gain changes several times over the range. A fixed gain is only good near its design length, so I solved the Riccati equation at several lengths and fitted each gain element with a polynomial, then clamped the leg length to the fitted range online.
>
> **Why VMC.** The legs must be compliant and force-controlled, because the balance controller produces torques, not positions. VMC lets me define a virtual spring and damper along the leg, add gravity feedforward, and convert the desired force to joint torques by the Jacobian transpose, which follows from virtual work. I also handled roll with opposite force corrections on the two legs, so total support stays the same.
>
> **Debugging.** The hardest part was not the equations but the real robot. Sign conventions and zero offsets gave inconsistent states; torque limits made the controller lose its linear region; and ground-contact transitions switched the dynamics. I added checks, rate limits and mode logic for each. The result is that the robot balanced and followed velocity commands during leg-length changes.
>
> （把你的真实调试故事替换进最后一段：【待你补全】。）

## 2. STAR 案例（用你真实经历替换细节）

### 案例 A：状态不一致（State inconsistency）

- **S**：Robot balance was unstable on the real hardware although the controller was stable in the model.
- **T**：Find out why and make the states trustworthy.
- **A**：I checked each state against a hand test: tilt the body, move one joint, push the wheel. I found that a sign or zero offset in some state (for example the pitch or the leg angle) did not match the model convention, and that the IMU and the joint feedback were sampled at different times. I unified the conventions, calibrated the zero offsets, and used a consistent snapshot per cycle.
- **R**：The estimated states matched the model and the balance became consistent.（具体哪个量【待你补全】）

### 案例 B：力矩限幅（Torque limits）

- **S**：Under larger disturbances or speed commands the robot suddenly lost balance.
- **T**：Understand the role of saturation.
- **A**：I logged the outputs and saw the hip or wheel torque hitting its limit; the effective gain dropped and the loop left its linear region. I ramped the velocity command, limited the leg-length rate, retuned Q and R to reduce torque demand, and added anti-windup.
- **R**：The robot tolerated larger commands without losing balance.

### 案例 C：离地切换（Ground-contact transition）

- **S**：The robot misbehaved when a wheel lost contact or when it landed.
- **T**：Detect contact and switch control safely.
- **A**：I estimated the support force on the wheel, used a threshold with debouncing, set the wheel torque and position feedback to a safe mode in the air, and reset the integrators and the position on landing.
- **R**：Contact transitions no longer caused torque spikes.

（以上三案例是对 CV 三类异常的展开；每个必须换成你真实发生的细节，否则面试时用"typical"措辞。）

## 3. 问答

### A. 总览

**1. What is a wheel-legged robot and why not a wheeled or legged one?**
> It combines wheel speed and efficiency with leg height adjustment and terrain adaptation. The cost is active balance and variable dynamics.
追问：*What are its weaknesses?* — 控制复杂，接触切换，能耗在站立时。

**2. Describe your control architecture.**
> Gain-scheduled LQR for balance, leg-length PID plus feedforward for support force, VMC to joint torques, FDCAN to the motors.

**3. What was your personal contribution?**
> Balance algorithms and on-robot tuning: the state construction, the scheduled LQR, the VMC, and the debugging. The mechanical platform was provided by the lab.

**4. What are the inputs and outputs of your LQR?**
> Six states in, two torques out: wheel-hub torque and hip torque.

**5. What did you achieve?**
> The robot kept its body balanced and tracked velocity commands while the leg length changed.
追问：*Numbers?* — 见第 7 节模板。

### B. 运动学

**6. How do you compute the foot position from the motor angles?**
> The motor angles give two joint points directly; the foot is the intersection of two circles around them with radii equal to the passive link lengths.

**7. Why two solutions, and how do you choose?**
> Two circles intersect twice, giving two assembly modes. I pick the one that matches the mounting, the wheel below the hip line, and for our dimensions the two modes never merge physically.

**8. How do you do inverse kinematics?**
> Two independent circle intersections: each active joint lies on the circle around the hip with the active link length and the circle around the foot with the passive link length. Four candidates, one chosen by angle range.

**9. What is the Jacobian here and how do you derive it?**
> It maps joint rates to foot velocity. Each passive link has constant length, so the velocity components along the link at both ends are equal. That gives two linear equations, which I solve for the foot velocity.

**10. What is a singularity of the five-bar?**
> Where the Jacobian loses rank: the passive links collinear, or an active link collinear with its passive link at full extension. I keep the leg length away from it.

**11. What is the virtual leg?**
> The line from the hip midpoint to the wheel axle. Its length and angle reparametrize the two degrees of freedom.

**12. How do you compute leg length rate and angle rate?**
> Project the foot velocity on the leg direction for the length rate, and take the perpendicular part divided by the length for the angle rate, via the Jacobian.

**13. How do you get the leg angle used in control?**
> World-frame leg angle: IMU pitch plus the leg angle relative to the body, measured from the vertical. The sign convention is checked on the robot.

### C. 建模与 LQR

**14. How did you model the robot?**
> A sagittal-plane three-body inverted pendulum: wheel, leg, body. Lagrange equations, motor torques as generalized forces by virtual work, linearized around upright.

**15. Why is the model linear and when does it break?**
> Small-angle linearization; it breaks for large tilt, slip, saturation, and fast leg-length changes.

**16. What does Q and R mean?**
> Q penalizes state error, R penalizes control effort; only their ratio matters.

**17. How did you choose Q and R?**
> Start with Bryson's rule, one over the square of the acceptable value, then adjust on the robot watching torque saturation and oscillation. 具体数值【待你补全】。

**18. What happens if you increase the pitch weight?**
> The body becomes stiffer, but the hip torque demand rises roughly in proportion.

**19. Is LQR robust?**
> For continuous-time LQR with full state feedback it has good guaranteed margins in theory, but delay, saturation and model error erode them, which is why I tuned on hardware.

**20. Why is the sign of the velocity gain counterintuitive?**
> Non-minimum phase: to slow down you must first accelerate the wheel so that the body leans back.

**21. How do you handle tracking a velocity command with LQR?**
> Use the error to the reference in the state, integrate the velocity command into a position reference, and ramp the command.

**22. Why not integral action?**
> LQR has none by itself; I rely on the position and velocity terms and feedforward, and could augment the state if needed. 你实机是否加积分【待你补全】。

**23. Continuous or discrete design?**
> Continuous design is fine when the sample time is much smaller than the fastest closed-loop time constant; otherwise use discrete LQR on the ZOH model.

### D. 增益调度

**24. Why schedule by leg length?**
> The plant depends on it: mass matrix and gravity term change, the unstable pole scales as one over the square root of length, and the input gain changes several times.

**25. How did you build K(L)?**
> Riccati at a grid of lengths, fit each element with a polynomial, check stability on a dense grid, clamp the length online.

**26. How many coefficients?**
> Twelve elements; with cubic fits that is 48 numbers. 你的实际阶数【待你补全】。

**27. Why polynomials instead of a lookup table?**
> Smooth, small, fixed compute; tables need interpolation and have derivative jumps at nodes. Both are valid.

**28. What if the leg length leaves the fitted range?**
> Extrapolation error grows, so I clamp it. In my teaching example one gain at 0.5 m was off by almost half.

**29. Is gain scheduling guaranteed stable?**
> Not in general, because the frozen-time analysis ignores the rate of change. So I limit the leg-length rate and validate on the robot.

**30. What would you do for a better scheme?**
> LPV or robust design, or MPC with the leg length as a measured parameter.

### E. VMC

**31. What is VMC?**
> Define desired force by a virtual spring-damper and map it to joint torques with the Jacobian transpose.

**32. Why the transpose?**
> Power balance: tau transpose times joint rate equals force transpose times foot velocity for all rates.

**33. How do you map support force and hip torque to joints?**
> Generalized forces on the leg length and angle; compose the foot Jacobian with the polar map and transpose.

**34. Why is VMC better than position control here?**
> It matches the force outputs of the balance loop and gives compliance against ground impact.

**35. What happens in the symmetric pose?**
> The support force becomes a differential joint torque and the hip torque a common-mode one.

**36. Numerical sense of the torques?**
> Teaching estimate: about 0.1 m/rad Jacobian entry and 30 N support gives about 3 N·m per hip joint. 实机值【待你补全】。

**37. What is the feedforward in the support force?**
> About half the body weight divided by the cosine of the leg angle.

**38. How do you handle roll?**
> Add support force to one leg and subtract from the other, so total support is unchanged.

**39. What prevents the legs from splitting?**
> A differential PD on the leg angles added to the hip torques, since LQR only sees the average.

**40. Units trap?**
> mm versus m in the Jacobian; I write units in the code and check a static case.

### F. 估计与接触

**41. How do you estimate velocity?**
> Absolute wheel rate plus leg motion terms, fused with IMU acceleration in a Kalman filter.

**42. Why fuse?**
> Wheel velocity has no drift but fails on slip; integrated acceleration is smooth but drifts.

**43. How to handle slip?**
> Monitor the innovation, inflate the measurement noise, limit torque.

**44. How to remove gravity from the accelerometer?**
> Use the pitch estimate: horizontal acceleration is about the axis reading plus g times pitch.

**45. How do you detect that a wheel left the ground?**
> Estimate the ground force from the support force, hip torque, wheel weight and vertical acceleration; below a threshold with debouncing means airborne.

**46. What do you do in the air?**
> Zero the wheel torque and the position and velocity feedback, keep the leg length and posture under control, and limit the support force.

**47. What on landing?**
> Reset position and integrators, ramp the gains back, and avoid torque steps.

**48. Why debounce?**
> Bumps momentarily reduce the force and would cause false switches.

### G. 嵌入式

**49. Describe the software structure.**
> FreeRTOS tasks: IMU, control at a fixed period, CAN receive via interrupt, communication and safety monitor. 实际划分【待你补全】。

**50. How do you share data between tasks?**
> Atomic snapshots or mutex-protected structures, timestamps for freshness.

**51. What is the FDCAN frame for the motors?**
> Typically an 8-byte frame with 16-bit position and 12-bit velocity, Kp, Kd and torque fields scaled between limits. 你的电机协议【待你补全】。

**52. Why quantization matters?**
> A 12-bit torque over ±18 has a resolution of about 0.009 in those units, and the command is clamped to the range.

**53. Bus load?**
> A classic 8-byte frame is about 110 to 135 bits, so at 1 Mbps eight frames per cycle take about 0.9 to 1.1 ms: that limits the rate unless FD with a faster data phase is used.

**54. What if CAN feedback stops?**
> Detect stale data by counters; command zero torque or a safe mode.

**55. How do you choose the control period?**
> Fast enough versus the closed-loop poles and delays, limited by the bus and the compute. 数值【待你补全】。

### H. 调试

**56. Name three classes of failure you found.**
> State inconsistency, torque limits, ground-contact transitions.

**57. First thing you check when it falls?**
> Signs and zero offsets with hand tests, then log states and torques.

**58. How do you know it is saturation?**
> Torque output sits at the limit while the state diverges; gains effectively drop.

**59. How do you tune safely?**
> Lift the robot, start with the leg loop, then low gains on the ground with a catch.

**60. What would you improve?**
> Better contact estimation, an adaptive or robust scheme, and systematic parameter identification.

### I. 个人

**61. What was hardest?**
> Making the real states agree with the model; the math was clean, the signs and timing were not.

**62. What did you learn?**
> Model, estimate, then control: most failures were in the states and limits, not in the gains.

**63. How is it related to your other projects?**
> P1 is the same inverted-pendulum idea on a ballbot; P3 and P2 share the embedded CAN execution chain.

## 4. LQR 数学深挖

**Derive the Riccati equation.**
> Assume the value function is s transpose P s. The Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman condition minimizes s'Qs + u'Ru + 2s'P(As+Bu) over u; setting the derivative to zero gives u = −R⁻¹B'P s. Substituting gives A'P + PA − PBR⁻¹B'P + Q = 0. The value function acts as a Lyapunov function with derivative −(s'Qs + u'Ru), so the closed loop is stable.

**Scalar example.** a=1, b=1, q=1, r=1: p=1+√2, K=2.414, pole −1.414. With r=0.1: K=4.317, pole −3.317.

**Double integrator.** Q=I, R=1: K=[1, √3], poles −0.866±0.5j.

**Conditions.** Stabilizable (A,B), detectable (Q^½, A). My six-state model is controllable (rank 6) for 0.1–0.3 m in the teaching example.

**Why K is 2×6.** Two inputs, six states; each row is one input's gains; both inputs see all states.

## 5. VMC 数学深挖

**Jacobian.** Link constraint: u₁·Ḃ = L₁ sin(θ₁−α) α̇, u₂·Ḃ = L₄ sin(θ₂−β) β̇ ⇒ M_c Ḃ = D_c q̇ ⇒ J = M_c⁻¹D_c, with det M_c = sin(θ₂−θ₁).

**Torque map.** τ = Jᵀ F by virtual work; virtual-leg form τ = J_pᵀ[F; T_p] with J_p = J_r J.

**Example.** α=135°, β=45°: J = [[−116.66, −116.66], [−97.24, 97.24]] mm/rad; L₀ = 298.1 mm; J_pᵀ rows (m units) [−0.0972, 0.3913], [0.0972, 0.3913]; F=40 N gives ∓3.89 N·m.

## 6. 为什么增益要按腿长调度（一分钟答法）

> Because A(L) and B(L) change. Mass matrix entries scale with L and L squared and the gravity term with L, so the leg-wheel unstable pole scales like 1/√L (15.5 to 26.7 for 0.3 to 0.1 m in my model) and the wheel-torque-to-leg-angle gain changes about fourfold. A gain designed at 0.2 m slows convergence by about 40% at 0.1 m and is unstable at 0.4 m in the model. So I compute K at several lengths and interpolate with polynomials.

（数字来自第 4、5 章教学模型，说"in a simplified model I used for design"。）
